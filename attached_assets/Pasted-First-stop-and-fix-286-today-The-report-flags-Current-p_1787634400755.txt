What the audit did to my earlier plan

You cannot test the Asia copy. server/index.ts:67-88 — in production, any *.replit.app request is 301-redirected to your custom domain. So the moment you publish the copy, visiting its own URL bounces you to the old site. My Phase 2 ("test on the .replit.app URL before touching DNS") is impossible until follow-up #288 is done.

An unguarded copy can destroy your live lead data. This is the finding that matters most. The walk-in reverse sync (walkinSheets.ts:2291-2325) starts 30 seconds after boot, then runs every 60 seconds, and it "archives missing rows and rewrites brand sheets."

Read that again. A copy booted with real Sheets credentials and a restored-but-stale database will, within half a minute and unprompted, rewrite your live RIS and RPS WALKINs tabs from its own stale data — and those tabs feed your Master Tracker through IMPORTRANGE. That's not a duplicate-rows problem. That's your admissions pipeline overwritten, with no undo.

The Agent confirmed there is no safe mode to prevent this (#287 proposes building one).

My recommendation: don't do this migration now

Weigh what's actually on each side.

Gain: roughly 300–500ms off TTFB. LCP from 2,453ms to maybe 2,000ms. No ranking change — same domain, same URLs, .in ccTLD already handling geo-targeting.

Cost: build a safe mode (#287), centralize host config across ~20 files touching canonicals, sitemap, robots, SSR metadata and monitoring (#288), fix a security exposure (#286), migrate a database with sequence preservation, relocate blog images off ephemeral disk, re-register OAuth redirect URIs across Google Cloud Console and MCP, reauthorize 19 MCP OAuth clients and 17 refresh tokens, run a write-freeze, and reconnect your Claude connectors — all on a system running live admissions, executed by someone who isn't a developer.

That's weeks of engineering risk for 400 milliseconds. The Agent reached the same conclusion independently: "Do not execute the regional move immediately."

Do this instead, in order
#286 today. Security, not optional.
Check whether your blog images are already broken. The audit found blog uploads live on local disk at ./uploads/blog, not Object Storage — and Autoscale filesystems aren't durable. With 96 blog posts and frequent republishes, some images may already be gone. Open five older blog posts and look. If they're broken, that's a live bug worth more than the migration.
The image optimization from my first message. Still your best speed lever — 1,243ms of image discovery and download time, versus 400ms from the region move, at a fraction of the risk.
#288, host centralization. Worth doing on its own merits — it removes hardcoded-domain fragility and makes any future move trivial.
Re-measure Core Web Vitals in four weeks. If LCP is comfortably under 2,000ms by then, the region move has no remaining justification.
