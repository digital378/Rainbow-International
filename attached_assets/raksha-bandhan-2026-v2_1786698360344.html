Chapter One

A promise, tied in silk

On Shravana Purnima — when the full moon rises over a month of rain — a sister ties a thread around her brother's wrist. It is a small thing: a few strands of silk, a bead, a knot.

And yet it carries one of the oldest promises we know. I am with you. I will look out for you. No ceremony in the world says so much with so little.

Chapter Two

More than a ritual

Raksha means protection. Bandhan means bond. Together, they remind us that love is not only felt — it is promised, renewed and acted upon, year after year.

The rakhi is delicate by design. Real strength, it teaches us, lies not in the thread itself, but in the intention behind it.

Chapter Three

Every classroom has its threads

At Rainbow International School, we see rakhis everywhere — not only on wrists. In the friend who saves a seat. In the senior who steadies a nervous newcomer. In every teacher who protects a child's confidence as carefully as their safety.

These invisible threads of care, respect and belonging are what let children learn fearlessly — and grow into their fullest selves.

Mark the Date

When is Raksha Bandhan 2026? Date & Significance

Raksha Bandhan 2026 will be celebrated on Friday, 28 August 2026, on Shravana Purnima — the full-moon day of the Hindu month of Shravan. It is one of India's most loved festivals, celebrating the bond of protection between brothers and sisters.

Friday, 28 August 2026
Shravana Purnima · Rakhi Purnima

On this day, a sister ties a rakhi — a sacred thread — on her brother's wrist, applies a tilak on his forehead, and prays for his wellbeing. In return, the brother gives her a gift and promises to protect and support her, always. Over the years the festival has grown beyond siblings: cousins, close friends, and even classmates tie rakhis as a mark of trust and affection.

As per traditional panchang calculations, the Purnima tithi is active at sunrise on 28 August and continues until mid-morning — around 9:48 AM IST — making the morning hours the preferred window for tying the rakhi. Since muhurat timings can vary by location and tradition, we encourage families to confirm with their local panchang.

Stories to Tell

The Story of Raksha Bandhan for Kids: Three Timeless Legends

Why do we celebrate Raksha Bandhan? Ask this at home and you may hear a different story from every grandparent — and that is the beauty of it. Here are three legends, retold simply for young listeners.

Krishna and Draupadi

Legend says that when Lord Krishna once hurt his finger, Draupadi did not hesitate — she tore a strip from her own sari and bound his wound. Touched by her instant, unthinking care, Krishna vowed to protect her whenever she needed him — a promise he honoured in her darkest hour. Many believe this simple act of love was the first rakhi.

Yama and Yamuna

In another tale, Yamuna tied a sacred thread on the wrist of her brother Yama, the god of death, who was so moved by her devotion that he granted her immortality — and declared that any brother whose sister ties a rakhi on this day shall be blessed with a long life.

Rani Karnavati and Humayun

History, too, has its rakhi story. It is widely retold that Rani Karnavati of Mewar, facing an invasion, sent a rakhi to Emperor Humayun seeking his protection. Honouring the thread from a woman he had never met, the emperor marched to her aid. The story is remembered as a reminder that the rakhi's promise can cross every boundary.

Ready for the Assembly

Raksha Bandhan Speech in English for Students

Preparing for a school assembly or class presentation? Here are two ready-to-use speeches — a short one for our younger speakers and a fuller one for secondary students. Practise aloud, speak slowly, and let the meaning of the festival shine through.

1-Minute Speech · Primary Students

Good morning respected teachers and my dear friends. Today I want to talk about a beautiful festival — Raksha Bandhan. Raksha means protection, and bandhan means bond. On this day, sisters tie a colourful thread called a rakhi on their brothers' wrists. The rakhi may be small, but its promise is big: "I will always care for you." Brothers give gifts and promise to protect their sisters. But Raksha Bandhan is not only for brothers and sisters. It teaches all of us to care for the people around us — our family, our friends, and our classmates. This year, Raksha Bandhan falls on 28 August. Let us celebrate it with love, sweets, and lots of smiles. Thank you!

2–3 Minute Speech · Secondary Students

Good morning respected Principal, teachers, and dear friends. Today I have the honour of speaking about Raksha Bandhan — a festival that captures, in a single thread, what it means to care for another person.

Celebrated on Shravana Purnima — this year on Friday, 28 August — Raksha Bandhan literally means "the bond of protection." A sister ties a rakhi on her brother's wrist and prays for his long life; the brother, in turn, promises to stand by her through every difficulty. Our epics and history are rich with its stories: Draupadi's torn sari binding Krishna's wound, Yamuna's thread on Yama's wrist, Rani Karnavati's rakhi that moved an emperor.

But I believe the festival's true message reaches far beyond siblings. Look around this hall. Every day, in small ways, we tie invisible rakhis — when we stand up for a friend, include someone who feels left out, or help a junior find their way. Protection is not only a promise made at home; it is a value we practise in our corridors and classrooms.

The rakhi is fragile — a few strands of silk. And that is precisely its lesson: strength does not lie in the thread, but in the intention behind it. This Raksha Bandhan, let us tie our threads with pride, and more importantly, let us live their promise every single day. Thank you, and a very happy Raksha Bandhan to you all!

For Your Notebook

Raksha Bandhan Essay in English & 10 Lines for Students

Essay · ~300 Words

Raksha Bandhan is one of the most cherished festivals of India, celebrated on Shravana Purnima, the full-moon day of the month of Shravan. The name comes from two Sanskrit words — raksha, meaning protection, and bandhan, meaning bond. In 2026, the festival falls on Friday, 28 August.

On this day, sisters tie a decorative sacred thread called a rakhi on their brothers' wrists. The ritual is simple and beautiful: an aarti is performed, a tilak is applied on the brother's forehead, the rakhi is tied, and sweets are shared. The brother gives his sister a gift and, more importantly, a promise — to protect her and stand beside her throughout life.

The festival is rooted in timeless stories. Draupadi once tore a strip of her sari to bandage Lord Krishna's injured finger, and Krishna vowed to protect her forever. In another legend, Yamuna's rakhi so moved her brother Yama that he blessed all brothers who honour the thread. History remembers Rani Karnavati, whose rakhi persuaded Emperor Humayun to ride to her kingdom's defence.

Today, Raksha Bandhan has grown beyond the bond of brothers and sisters. Cousins, friends, and neighbours tie rakhis as symbols of trust, gratitude, and friendship. In schools, children make rakhis by hand and learn the festival's deeper meaning — that caring for others is a responsibility we all share.

Raksha Bandhan reminds us that the strongest bonds are often expressed through the simplest gestures. A thread costs little, yet the promise it carries is priceless. That is why, year after year, this gentle festival continues to bind families — and hearts — together.

Make, Play, Celebrate

Rakhi Making Ideas & Raksha Bandhan Activities for Kids

The sweetest rakhis are the ones made at home. Here are five simple, joyful activities parents and children can do together this festival week — no fancy materials needed.

1

Thread-and-Bead Rakhi

Braid three strands of silk thread or wool, then string on colourful beads and knot a larger bead at the centre. Simple enough for little fingers, beautiful enough to wear with pride.

2

Paper-Quilling Rakhi

Roll thin strips of coloured paper into tight coils, pinch them into petal shapes, and glue them onto a circle of card. Attach a ribbon — and you have a rakhi that doubles as a work of art.

3

Promise Cards

Alongside the rakhi, have each sibling write one real, keepable promise on a small card — "I will help you with maths," "I will water your plant when you forget." Read them aloud after the ceremony. These cards often become keepsakes.

4

Cook a Sweet Together

Pick one easy festive sweet — coconut ladoos or kheer work wonderfully — and cook it as a team. Children remember the flour-dusted laughter far longer than the sweet itself.

5

Gratitude Circle

Before the sweets are eaten, sit in a circle and let every family member share one thing they are grateful for about the person on their left. Two minutes, no phones — a small ritual with a big glow.

Raksha Bandhan 2026: Frequently Asked Questions

When is Raksha Bandhan in 2026?
Raksha Bandhan 2026 falls on Friday, 28 August 2026, on Shravana Purnima — the full-moon day of the Hindu month of Shravan.
Why do we celebrate Raksha Bandhan?
The festival celebrates the protective bond between siblings. A sister ties a rakhi on her brother's wrist as a symbol of love, and the brother promises to protect and support her. Over time it has grown into a wider celebration of care between siblings, cousins and close friends.
What is the story behind Raksha Bandhan?
Several legends are associated with the festival — Draupadi tying a strip of her sari on Lord Krishna's wrist, the devotion between Yamuna and her brother Yama, and the historical account of Rani Karnavati sending a rakhi to Emperor Humayun. Each story carries the same message: a small thread, a mighty promise.
What time should the rakhi be tied in 2026?
As per traditional panchang calculations, the Purnima tithi is active at sunrise on 28 August and continues until mid-morning, around 9:48 AM IST — so the morning is the preferred window. Timings can vary by location and tradition, so please confirm with your local panchang.
What does Raksha Bandhan mean?
In Sanskrit, raksha means protection and bandhan means bond or tie. Raksha Bandhan literally means "the bond of protection."
How can schools celebrate Raksha Bandhan?
Schools can celebrate with rakhi-making craft sessions, storytelling of the festival's legends, promise-card and gratitude activities, cultural assemblies, and classroom discussions on the values of care, respect and protection that the festival represents.
