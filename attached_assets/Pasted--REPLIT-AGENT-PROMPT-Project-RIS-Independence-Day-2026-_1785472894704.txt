### SECTION 4: NATIONAL SYMBOLS

**Section id:** `symbols`

**H2:** India's National Symbols

**Intro:** India's national symbols represent the country's rich heritage, culture, and values. Adopted after independence, these symbols unite 1.4 billion Indians across all languages, religions, and regions.

**Image:** `img-05-india-map-mandala.jpg` — featured image for this section, full-width, caption: *"India — one nation, many cultures, one identity"*

**6 Symbol Cards** (3-column grid on desktop, 2-col tablet, 1-col mobile):

**Card 1 — National Flag**
Icon: 🏳️ (use an SVG tricolor flag icon or CSS tricolor)
Name: **Tiranga (The Tricolour)**
Fact: Three horizontal bands — Saffron (top): courage & sacrifice; White (middle): peace & truth; Green (bottom): faith & prosperity. The 24-spoked navy Ashoka Chakra in the center represents the eternal wheel of dharma (righteousness). Designed by Pingali Venkayya.

**Card 2 — National Anthem**
Icon: 🎵
Name: **Jana Gana Mana**
Fact: Written by Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore; officially adopted as national anthem on January 24, 1950. The anthem consists of 5 stanzas; only the first stanza is sung officially. Full duration: 52 seconds.

**Card 3 — National Animal**
Icon: 🐯
Name: **Bengal Tiger** (Panthera tigris tigris)
Fact: Declared national animal in 1973 under Project Tiger, replacing the lion. The Bengal Tiger symbolises strength, grace, and power. India is home to over 70% of the world's wild tiger population.

**Card 4 — National Bird**
Icon: 🦚
Name: **Indian Peacock** (Pavo cristatus)
Fact: Declared national bird in 1963. Known for its spectacular plumage and courtship dance, the peacock is a symbol of beauty, grace, and pride. Sacred in Indian mythology, associated with the god Kartikeya.

**Card 5 — National Flower**
Icon: 🌸
Name: **Lotus** (Nelumbo nucifera)
Fact: Symbol of purity, beauty, wealth, and knowledge. Revered in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. The lotus rises from muddy water to bloom in sunlight — a metaphor for India's growth.

**Card 6 — National Fruit**
Icon: 🥭
Name: **Mango** (Mangifera indica)
Fact: Cultivated in India for over 4,000 years. India is the world's largest producer of mangoes. The mango is associated with love, fertility, and prosperity in Indian culture.

#### TAB 4 — English: 500 Words

India's Independence Day is not merely a public holiday — it is the celebration of a miracle. On August 15, 1947, a civilisation that had endured centuries of struggle, subjugation, and sacrifice finally breathed free. A nation of extraordinary diversity — in language, faith, culture, and geography — emerged from colonial rule to declare, in one collective voice: we are free.

In 2026, as India observes its 80th Independence Day, we stand at a moment of profound reflection. Eighty years ago, India was a newly born nation — fragile yet full of promise. Today, she is the world's largest democracy, a global economic power, a space-faring nation, and home to 1.4 billion citizens, each carrying within them the spirit of independence that our forefathers fought and died for.

The road to independence was neither short nor easy. India's freedom struggle spans nearly a century of organised resistance. The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, became the platform through which leaders like Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, and Bipin Chandra Pal galvanised public opinion against British rule. When Mahatma Gandhi returned to India in 1915, the movement transformed into a mass awakening — one rooted in truth (satya) and non-violence (ahimsa).

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 1919 shook the nation to its core and turned countless Indians from passive subjects into active freedom fighters. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920), the Dandi March (1930), and the Quit India Movement (1942) were waves of defiance that Britain could neither crush nor ignore. Revolutionaries like Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, and Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose showed the world that India's youth were not afraid to sacrifice their lives for freedom.

On the night of August 14, 1947, as India's first Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru addressed the Constituent Assembly, history held its breath. "At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom." With those words, a new era began. The Tiranga was hoisted for the first time over Red Fort, and millions wept tears of joy across the subcontinent.

Today, as we celebrate 80 years of independence, we celebrate more than a date on the calendar. We celebrate the vision of Dr B R Ambedkar, who gave India its Constitution — a document that guaranteed every citizen fundamental rights and equality before law. We celebrate the quiet courage of ordinary Indians — farmers, teachers, labourers, mothers, and students — who built this nation from the ground up.

Independence Day at Rainbow International School, Thane, is a day of learning and patriotism. Our students recite speeches, perform plays, and participate in flag-hoisting ceremonies — not just as a ritual, but as a living connection to the values our nation holds dear. We believe that true independence is not only political — it is the freedom to think, to dream, to create, and to contribute to a just and equal society.

As we unfurl the Tiranga this August 15, let each of us ask: What are we doing with the freedom our ancestors gifted us? The best tribute to our freedom fighters is not nostalgia — it is action. It is becoming the citizens, the leaders, and the builders that India needs for its next 80 years.

Jai Hind! Jai Bharat! 🇮🇳

**H2:** Tryst with Destiny — Nehru's Midnight Speech

**Body:**
On the eve of India's independence — the night of August 14, 1947 — Jawaharlal Nehru stood before the Constituent Assembly of India and delivered what is considered one of the greatest speeches in the history of human freedom. Broadcast live on All India Radio, it was heard by millions of Indians clustered around radios in homes, streets, and public squares.

**Pull Quote (styled prominently):**
> "At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom. A moment comes, which comes but rarely in history, when we step out from the old to the new, when an age ends, and when the soul of a nation, long suppressed, finds utterance...
>
> "We end today a period of ill fortune and India discovers herself again. The achievement we celebrate today is but a step, an opening of opportunity, to the greater triumphs and achievements that await us. Are we brave enough and wise enough to grasp this opportunity and accept the challenge of the future?"
>
> — Jawaharlal Nehru, addressing the Constituent Assembly of India, August 14–15, 1947

**Body continued:**
These words — delivered in perfect English at the stroke of midnight — became the founding declaration of independent India. Nehru spoke not just of political freedom, but of a nation's destiny: the responsibility to build a just, secular, democratic republic. The speech lasted approximately 13 minutes and concluded with "Jai Hind!" — a phrase that has echoed across every Independence Day since.

On this 80th anniversary, Nehru's words resonate with fresh urgency. India has honored that tryst — imperfectly, humanly, but persistently — for eight decades. The work continues.

**10 Facts:**

1. 🕛 **Midnight Miracle** — India gained independence at exactly 12:00 midnight on August 15, 1947. Jawaharlal Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny" speech was delivered to the Constituent Assembly at that precise moment, making India one of the few nations to be born at midnight.

2. 🏳️ **Designed by a Freedom Fighter** — The Indian national flag was designed by Pingali Venkayya, a freedom fighter and linguist from Andhra Pradesh. He presented over 30 flag designs to Mahatma Gandhi before the final design was approved in 1921.

3. 🔵 **24 Hours, 24 Spokes** — The Ashoka Chakra in the Indian flag has exactly 24 spokes — one for each hour of the day. It symbolises that the law (dharma) is always moving and that the nation must never stand still.

4. 🎵 **Nobel Laureate's Song** — "Jana Gana Mana" was composed by Rabindranath Tagore, who won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1913. It was officially adopted as India's national anthem on January 24, 1950, by the Constituent Assembly.

6. 🌏 **World's Largest Democracy** — India is the world's largest democracy with over 960 million registered voters. General elections in India involve more voters than the combined populations of the United States and Europe.

7. 📜 **Longest Written Constitution** — India's Constitution, which came into force on January 26, 1950 (now celebrated as Republic Day), is the longest written constitution of any sovereign nation in the world — originally consisting of 395 articles, 8 schedules, and 22 parts.

8. 📻 **Radio's Greatest Broadcast** — Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny" speech was the first live broadcast on All India Radio to be heard by millions of Indians simultaneously — a technological milestone as significant as the political one.

9. 🚀 **From Independence to Moon** — India launched its Chandrayaan-3 mission in 2023, becoming only the fourth country to land on the Moon — and the first to land near the lunar south pole. From 1947's freedom to Moon exploration in 76 years — India's journey has been extraordinary.

10. 🌺 **Lotus Nation** — India's national flower, the Lotus, blooms in muddy water and rises to perfection above the surface — a metaphor that the nation's founders consciously chose to represent India's spirit: rising above adversity to achieve greatness.

**Q1.** When does India celebrate Independence Day?

**Q2.** Which Independence Day anniversary is India celebrating in 2026?
**A:** India's **80th Independence Day**. Counting 1947 as the 1st, 2026 (1947 + 79 = 2026) is the 80th anniversary.

**Q3.** Who delivered the famous "Tryst with Destiny" speech?
**A:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**, India's first Prime Minister. He delivered it at midnight on August 14–15, 1947, to the Constituent Assembly of India. The speech was broadcast live on All India Radio.

**Q4.** What does the Ashoka Chakra on the Indian flag represent?
**A:** It represents the **eternal wheel of dharma (law and righteousness)** and the principle of progress — the idea that life must always move forward. It derives from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath.

**Q5.** How many spokes does the Ashoka Chakra have?
**A:** **24 spokes** — representing the 24 hours of the day, symbolising that the nation and its values must be active round the clock.

**Q6.** Who designed the Indian national flag?
**A:** **Pingali Venkayya**, a freedom fighter and linguist from Andhra Pradesh. He created the design in 1921; it was formally adopted on July 22, 1947.

**Q7.** What do the three colours of the Indian national flag represent?
**A:** **Saffron (Kesari)** — courage, sacrifice & the spirit of renunciation. **White** — peace, purity & truth. **Green** — faith, fertility & prosperity.

**Q8.** Who is known as the Father of the Nation?
**A:** **Mahatma Gandhi** (Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi), for his leadership of India's non-violent independence movement and his philosophy of Satyagraha (truth-force).

**Q9.** What was the Dandi March and when did it happen?
**A:** The **Dandi March (Salt Satyagraha)** was a 240-mile march led by Mahatma Gandhi from March 12 to April 5, **1930**, to protest the British salt tax. It sparked mass civil disobedience across India and drew global attention to the independence movement.

**Q10.** When was the Quit India Movement launched, and what was Gandhi's call?
**A:** Launched on **August 8, 1942**. Gandhi gave the battle cry: **"Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro)** — demanding that Britain leave India immediately or face a mass revolt. It was one of the largest mobilisations in Indian history.

**Q11.** What is India's national anthem?
**A:** **"Jana Gana Mana"** — written and composed by Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore. It was officially adopted as the national anthem on January 24, 1950.

**Q12.** Who wrote India's national anthem "Jana Gana Mana"?
**A:** **Rabindranath Tagore**, the first Asian Nobel laureate (Literature, 1913). He wrote it originally in Bengali; it was later adopted in its Sanskrit-inflected form as the national anthem.

**Q13.** What is India's national animal?
**A:** The **Bengal Tiger** (Panthera tigris tigris). It was declared the national animal in 1973 under Project Tiger, which was launched to protect the endangered species.

**Q14.** Fill in the blank: India is the world's ________ democracy.
**A:** **Largest**. India has over 960 million registered voters — more than the populations of the US and EU combined. General elections are held every 5 years.

**Q15.** Who was India's first Prime Minister?
**A:** **Jawaharlal Nehru**, who served as Prime Minister from August 15, 1947, until his death on May 27, 1964 — the longest-serving PM in India's history (nearly 17 years).

**B1.** What does "Vande Mataram" mean, and who wrote it?
**A:** "Vande Mataram" means **"I bow to thee, Mother"** — a salutation to the motherland. It was written by **Bankimchandra Chattopadhyay** in his 1882 novel *Anandamath*. It served as the rallying cry for India's independence movement and is now the national song (distinct from the national anthem).

**B3.** Who was the last Viceroy of India?

**B4.** What is the full name of the INC, and when was it founded?
**A:** **Indian National Congress**, founded in **1885** by A O Hume, Dadabhai Naoroji, and Dinshaw Wacha. It became the primary vehicle of India's independence movement under leaders including Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Gokhale, Gandhi, and Nehru.

**B5.** What does "Sarfaroshi ki Tamanna" mean, and who wrote it?
**A:** It means **"The desire / longing for sacrifice"** — referring to willingness to sacrifice one's life for the nation. The famous poem was written by **Ram Prasad Bismil**, a revolutionary freedom fighter, in 1921. The full line is: "Sarfaroshi ki tamanna ab hamare dil mein hai, dekhna hai zor kitna baazu-e-qaatil mein hai."

**Q2: Why is Independence Day celebrated on August 15?**
On August 15, 1947, India officially became independent from British colonial rule. Lord Louis Mountbatten, the last Viceroy of British India, transferred power to Indian leaders, and Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru hoisted the national flag at Red Fort in New Delhi for the first time. The celebration on August 15 commemorates this historic moment of liberation.

**Q3: What is India's 80th Independence Day in 2026?**
2026 marks the **80th anniversary** of India's independence. Counting from 1947 (the 1st Independence Day), the year 2026 is 1947 + 79 = 2026, making it the 80th. The 80th Independence Day is a landmark milestone and is being celebrated with special national programmes and commemorations.

**Q4: What is the significance of the Ashoka Chakra on the Indian flag?**
The Ashoka Chakra is a **24-spoked wheel** derived from the Lion Capital of Emperor Ashoka at Sarnath. It appears in navy blue at the center of the white band in the Indian flag. It represents the **eternal wheel of dharma (righteousness and law)** — symbolising that truth and virtue must always be in motion, and that India must never stand still in its pursuit of justice and progress.

**Q5: What do the three colours of the Indian flag mean?**
The Indian flag has three horizontal bands: **Saffron (Kesari)** at the top represents courage, sacrifice, and the spirit of renunciation; **White** in the middle represents peace, purity, and truth; **Green** at the bottom represents faith, fertility, and prosperity. Together, the three colours reflect India's values, aspirations, and diversity.

**Q6: How is Independence Day celebrated in schools?**
Schools celebrate Independence Day with **flag-hoisting ceremonies**, singing of the national anthem, patriotic songs, speeches by students and teachers, essay and elocution competitions, cultural programmes, skits on freedom fighters, and march-pasts. Many schools also distribute sweets and organise poster-making activities. At Rainbow International School, Thane, we celebrate with great enthusiasm and a deep sense of national pride.

**Q7: Who was the first person to hoist the Indian national flag at Red Fort?**
**Jawaharlal Nehru**, India's first Prime Minister, hoisted the Indian national flag at Red Fort (Lal Qila) in Delhi on **August 15, 1947**, marking India's first Independence Day. This tradition has continued unbroken every year since — each Prime Minister hoists the flag at Red Fort and addresses the nation on Independence Day.

**Q8: What is the "Tryst with Destiny" speech?**
"Tryst with Destiny" is the famous speech delivered by Jawaharlal Nehru to the Constituent Assembly of India at **midnight on August 14–15, 1947**, marking the moment of India's independence. The speech begins: *"At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom."* It is one of the most celebrated political speeches in modern history and defined the aspirations of independent India.

**Q9: How can students celebrate Independence Day at home?**
Students can celebrate at home by **watching the national flag-hoisting ceremony** on television, reading about freedom fighters and India's history, writing essays or practising speeches, creating patriotic artwork, sharing Independence Day greetings with family and friends, participating in community events, and watching patriotic films or documentaries about India's independence movement. Simple acts — like learning the full national anthem or reading Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny" speech — can be deeply meaningful.

  const text = panel.innerText.replace('📋 Copy Text', '').trim();

    showToast('Copied to clipboard! ✓');
